# Dispatch AI — MVP Prototype

A working prototype of the AI chat/call agent platform: a landing page with a live AI chat demo, a client intake form, an AI script generator, and a review/approve dashboard.

## What's actually working right now

The landing page (`/`) has a real chat widget wired to Claude, plus a visible pricing section. The intake form (`/onboard`) saves a new client and calls Claude to draft their agent's script from the answers. The dashboard (`/dashboard`) lists every client, lets you edit the draft script, approve it, and generate a real Stripe subscription payment link to send to that client. The dashboard is protected by a login (HTTP Basic Auth), and Stripe webhooks automatically keep each client's status in sync — flipping to "paid" the moment they complete checkout, and to "cancelled" the moment their subscription actually ends, with no manual checking required either way.

New signups no longer have to wait on you to review anything by hand. Right after a script is drafted, a second, independent AI pass (`lib/scriptSafetyCheckPrompt.js`) reads it back against that business's intake answers, specifically checking for the kinds of mistakes a human reviewer would catch — a price or policy that contradicts the intake answers, an implied service the business said they don't offer, leftover placeholder text, a missing AI-disclosure greeting, or important fields left blank. If it's confident nothing's wrong, the client is auto-approved on the spot and emailed a real Stripe payment link immediately — no waiting on you at all. If it's not confident, the client stays in manual "draft" review exactly like before this existed, and you get emailed right away with the AI's own plain-English reason, so nothing sits unnoticed just because you didn't happen to check the dashboard. Every failure mode in this path (a Claude error, Stripe not being configured yet, an email failing to send) fails safe back to manual review rather than ever risking a bad auto-approval — this is deliberately conservative, since you don't know each client's business well enough to catch mistakes yourself, so the AI check has to be the one erring on the side of caution.

Cancellation is self-service for your clients, not just you: after paying, a client lands on `/success`, which has a "Manage my subscription" button opening Stripe's own Customer Portal — from there they can cancel, update their card, or view invoices themselves, with no email or phone call to you required. You can also generate that same portal link for any paid client at any time from a button on their dashboard card, for cases where they reach out to you directly instead.

The intake form (`/onboard`) also has a small "Need help?" chat widget in the corner — a separate AI persona from the landing page demo, scoped to answering questions about the form itself, pricing, and what happens after signup, rather than pretending to be a customer's future AI agent.

Paying clients can now put a real, working AI chat widget on their *own* website, not just yours. Every paid client's dashboard card has a "Website chat widget code" box with a one-line embed snippet (`public/widget.js`, served publicly and built to run on any third-party site) -- paste it into their site and a chat bubble appears there, answering using that specific client's own founder-approved script. It's gated on `status === 'paid'` specifically, so it switches off automatically the moment a client cancels, the same way everything else here does. Right next to that embed code is a link to a new step-by-step install guide (`public/widget-guide.html`) with plain-English instructions split by platform -- WordPress, Wix, Squarespace, or "send this to whoever built your site" for anything else -- so a non-technical client isn't left guessing what to do with a raw code snippet.

Every one of those website widget conversations is now remembered, not just answered and forgotten. A paid client's dashboard card has a "Website chat conversations" section listing every conversation their widget has had, each one automatically tagged (once it's gone quiet for a few minutes) as **Booked**, **Unbooked lead**, **Question answered**, or **Not reviewed yet**, with any name/email/phone the visitor gave and a one-line summary -- plus the full transcript if you want to read it. This is step one of turning "we answer every call" into "we make sure no lead goes cold."

Step two is now built too: for any conversation tagged **Unbooked lead** where the visitor gave an email, the AI drafts a short, on-brand follow-up message (subject + body) referencing what they actually asked about. It shows up right under that conversation in the dashboard, fully editable, with "Save edits" and "Approve draft" buttons -- the same review-before-anything-goes-live pattern used for the main agent script.

A paying client's AI chat widget can now actually book a real appointment, not just talk about one. From a "Connect Google Calendar" button on their dashboard card, a client connects their own Google Calendar (a one-time Google sign-in and approval, no code). Once connected, their widget gets two extra abilities during a live chat: checking real availability on that calendar before offering a time, and creating a real calendar event once a customer agrees to one -- with a best-effort re-check right before writing, so two visitors chatting at once are unlikely to double-book the same slot. Every datetime is computed against the business's own time zone (a new field on the intake form) using Node's built-in `Intl` support, correctly handling daylight saving changes, rather than assuming the server's own time zone. If a client hasn't connected a calendar yet -- or if Google access ever fails or gets revoked -- the widget falls straight back to today's behavior: collecting the customer's preferred time and contact info and saying a team member will confirm it, never claiming something is booked unless it actually is. The founder's dashboard shows connection status per client, including a clear warning if a connection needs to be reconnected.

Step three closes the loop: once a draft is approved, a "Send now" button appears and actually emails it to the visitor, via Resend (`lib/emailClient.js`, plain `fetch`, no SDK). Approving and sending are deliberately two separate, founder-triggered clicks -- signing off on the wording doesn't automatically put an email in a real person's inbox. Until `RESEND_API_KEY` and `FOLLOWUP_FROM_EMAIL` are set (see `LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md` Step 9), "Send now" shows a demo-mode message instead of sending anything, the same fallback pattern used for Claude and Stripe elsewhere in this app.

Paying clients can now get a real, live phone number, entirely from your dashboard -- no command line needed. Once a client is on a paid plan, their card has a "Create phone agent" button that pushes their approved script into Retell AI (`lib/retellClient.js`, plain `fetch`, no SDK) as a real, callable voice agent. It's gated on `status === 'paid'` (not just "approved"), since a live phone number costs real money via Retell/Twilio, and it's safe to click more than once -- if an agent already exists for that client, it's reused rather than duplicated. Buying and assigning the actual phone number still happens inside Retell's own dashboard (that part genuinely needs a human clicking around Retell's UI), but once you have it, there's a box right there on the client's card to save it for reference. Until `RETELL_API_KEY` is set, the button shows a demo-mode message instead of creating anything real, the same fallback pattern used everywhere else here.

The minute allowances on the pricing page (250/750 call & chat minutes a month) are now real numbers something actually tracks and enforces, not just text on a page. Every real phone call (via Retell, once its webhook reports the call ended) and every website-widget chat message (a fixed estimate per message, since a chat doesn't have a natural "minute" the way a call does) adds to that client's usage for their current billing period. At 80% of their plan's allowance, the client gets an automatic heads-up email; past 100%, they get a second email explaining that extra minutes are now billed automatically, and every newly-crossed overage minute is added to their Stripe account as a "pending" invoice item -- Stripe attaches those to their next scheduled invoice on its own, so this needed no metered-billing product setup in the Stripe dashboard and no new environment variables at all. None of this ever blocks or slows down the AI itself -- going over a plan costs the client money, never a real customer a missed call or a stalled chat. Usage resets automatically at the start of each billing cycle, and your dashboard shows a live minutes-used bar (plus any overage) right on each paid client's card.

Clients can now log into their own self-service account instead of you generating and sending them a link for every single thing. From `/client-login`, a client enters the email they signed up with and gets a one-click emailed login link -- no password to set, forget, or reset (the same "long unguessable token" security model already used for the Stripe and past-customer portals, just reached through an emailed magic link the first time instead of one you send by hand). Once in, their portal (`public/client-portal.html`) covers everything on their dashboard card: editing their script, managing billing or resubscribing, creating a phone agent, connecting their calendar, reviewing chat conversations and approving follow-ups, checking their usage, and turning the hosted-website add-on below on or off. The one meaningful difference from your own dashboard: a client's script edit only goes live immediately if the exact same automatic safety check used on a brand-new signup (`checkScriptSafety` in `server.js`, shared with the onboarding flow) is confident it's safe -- anything else leaves their live script untouched and lands on your dashboard as a suggested edit to apply or discard, with a heads-up email, so a client can never accidentally put something wrong in front of a real customer. You can also generate a client's login link yourself from their dashboard card ("Get client login link") rather than waiting on them to request their own first login.

For any client without a website of their own -- or whose existing site won't let them add the chat widget -- there's now a hosted-website add-on, included free on any paid plan. Turning it on (from either their own portal or your dashboard) gives them a simple one-page site at `/site/<their-business-slug>` on this app's own domain: their business name, trade, hours, and phone number, plus the same AI chat embedded directly on the page instead of as a floating widget. It runs through the exact same `/api/widget-chat` route as the widget clients paste into their own sites, so it gets the same usage tracking, the same conversation storage and AI-drafted follow-ups, and the same real-time calendar booking -- tagged with a `hosted-page` source so it's distinguishable from their own-site conversations in their conversation list. No new setup, no new environment variables: it's just another toggle.

Reaching out to *past* customers for repeat or seasonal work is now built too, and it's the first feature clients handle entirely themselves rather than through you. A "Get customer upload link" button on any paid client's dashboard card generates a private, unguessable link (`public/customer-portal.html`, `lib/db.js`'s `pastCustomerPortalToken` -- the same "long token instead of a login" pattern already used for the Stripe self-service portal) that you send the client directly. There, they upload a CSV of their own past customers (name, email, last service date, and how many months until it's worth checking back in -- 6 months if left blank). Whenever enough time has passed since a customer's last service, the AI drafts a short, warm reminder (`lib/pastCustomerOutreachPrompt.js`) for the client to review, edit, approve, and send themselves -- deliberately reviewed by the client, not you, since they know their own past customers and you don't, the same reasoning behind the automatic script safety check. Your dashboard gets a read-only "View past-customer outreach" section so you can see what's happening without having to ask. CSV parsing (`lib/csv.js`) is hand-written, not a library, to keep this project's zero-dependency approach -- it handles quoted fields and commas inside them, which covers real exports from Excel, Google Sheets, and Numbers.

Everything runs as a single Node.js process with **zero npm dependencies** — no `npm install` step is required to run it, only Node.js itself (version 18 or newer).

Without an `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set, the app runs in "demo mode": every AI response is a canned placeholder, but the entire flow (forms, saving, dashboard, editing, approving) is fully testable. Without a `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` set, the payment link buttons show a demo-mode message instead of a real link. Both fallbacks, plus the dashboard login and the webhook signature verification, were tested end-to-end before this file was handed to you.

## Running it locally

1. Install Node.js 18+ if you don't have it (nodejs.org).
2. Open a terminal in this folder.
3. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` and fill in `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (get one at console.anthropic.com) to get real AI responses instead of placeholders.
4. Run `node server.js`.
5. Open `http://localhost:3000` in a browser.

## What's NOT built yet (by design, for an MVP)

There's no real database (client data, and now conversation data, live in JSON files — fine for testing and your first several dozen clients, not for large-scale simultaneous edits). Important: on Render, those files only survive restarts/redeploys once you've completed Step 8 in `LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md` (a paid plan plus a persistent disk) — without it, Render's free tier silently wipes client and conversation data on every restart, which is a real risk once you have actual customers, not just a testing inconvenience.

The "never let a lead go cold" follow-up idea is now fully built end to end for new leads: every website widget conversation gets stored and tagged, unbooked leads with an email get an AI-drafted follow-up you can edit and approve, and approved drafts can actually be emailed with one more click, once Resend is connected (Step 9 in `LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md`).

Automatic script approval currently always sends auto-approved signups a Starter plan payment link — there's no plan choice on the intake form itself, so if you want some signups to go straight to Growth, that still needs a manual "Get Growth link" click from the dashboard instead. The founder review-needed notification reuses `FOLLOWUP_REPLY_TO` (the same address used for lead follow-up replies) rather than a separate setting — if that's not set, flagged signups still show up in the dashboard, you just won't get an email nudge about them.

Creating a live phone agent is a dashboard button now, but taking it offline again isn't automatic yet: a cancelled subscription correctly flips a client's status to "cancelled," and the dashboard now shows their Retell agent ID (and saved number, if any) right on the cancelled card as a reminder, but actually pausing or removing that agent still needs a manual step in Retell's own dashboard -- a good next addition once this has been used for real a few times and the Retell API for that is worth wiring up.

Past-customer outreach has a few known rough edges worth knowing about. Re-uploading a CSV always adds new rows rather than updating or de-duplicating existing ones by name or email -- fine for a first upload, but uploading the same list twice will create duplicates, so tell clients to only add new customers on repeat uploads for now. "Due for a reminder" is a simple date-plus-months calculation, not anything trade-specific -- there's no built-in sense that an AC tune-up and a plumbing job have different natural intervals beyond whatever `remindAfterMonths` the client sets per row. And there's no automatic retry or delivery-failure handling if a send fails partway, here or anywhere else in this app -- it's a single attempt, and a real error message shows if it doesn't go through.

Real-time calendar booking currently only supports Google Calendar -- not Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, or other trade-specific field service software some clients may already use day to day (the intake form's "scheduling software" question is still just informational context for the script, not a real integration with any of those). It also assumes one calendar per business (their "primary" Google Calendar, no multi-technician or multi-calendar support) and one default appointment length per business, not per service type. The last-second double-booking check is best-effort, not a real lock, so a true simultaneous booking race is still possible in principle, just unlikely at MVP volume. And while the app is in Google's "Testing" publishing status (the default, and the right choice while starting out per LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md Step 10), each client's Google account has to be added as a test user before they can connect -- fine for the first several dozen clients, a real step to revisit only once you're growing well past that.

Usage tracking has a few known rough edges worth knowing about. Website-widget chat "minutes" are a fixed estimate (2 minutes per visitor message) rather than the real wall-clock length of a conversation -- simple and predictable, but it won't always match a chat's actual duration exactly, so don't expect it to line up minute-for-minute with a stopwatch. Real call duration relies on Retell's webhook payload matching the shape this was written against -- worth a real test call and a look at your Render logs the first time a client's phone agent goes live, in case Retell's exact fields have changed since. There's no view of *past* billing periods yet, only the current one, and no proration if a client changes plans partway through a period.

The dashboard login uses a single shared username/password rather than per-user accounts — fine for one founder, not for a team. Note that the self-service subscription portal itself requires a one-time toggle in Stripe's own dashboard (see LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md Step 6) — without it, the "Manage my subscription" button will show an error instead of opening the portal.

Client self-service login has a few known rough edges worth knowing about. There's no real session or "log out" -- a client's portal link works indefinitely (like the past-customer portal link before it), the only control is "get a new private link," which immediately invalidates the old one, meant for someone who thinks their link leaked rather than everyday use. Login is gated on having a real, matching contact email on a client that's actually subscribed at some point (paid or cancelled) -- a brand-new signup still in review has nothing to self-serve yet. Sending the actual login email needs Resend configured (Step 9 in `LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md`), the same as every other real email in this app; until then, the login link is returned directly in the API response instead so it's still testable.

The hosted-website add-on is deliberately a single simple page, not a real website builder -- one AI chat, business name, trade, hours, and phone number, pulled straight from the intake form with no further customization (no photos, no extra pages, no custom domain, no SEO beyond a page title). It's meant as a fallback for a client with nothing better, not a replacement for a real site once a business grows past needing one.

## File guide

`server.js` is the entire backend — routes, static file serving, dashboard auth, Stripe checkout and webhook handling, all in one file for easy reading. `lib/claude.js` calls the Anthropic API (and falls back to placeholder text if no key is set); it also optionally accepts Claude tool definitions, used by the widget-chat route's real-time booking tools. `lib/stripeClient.js` creates Stripe Checkout sessions, verifies webhook signatures, and creates the "pending invoice items" that power automatic overage billing, using plain `fetch` and Node's built-in `crypto` (no Stripe SDK dependency). `server.js`'s usage-tracking section (`recordUsageMinutes` and friends) is where a client's monthly call/chat minutes are actually counted, warned about, and billed for -- fed by the `/api/retell/webhook/:token` route (real call duration) and the widget-chat route (a fixed estimate per chat message). `lib/scriptPrompt.js` builds the prompt that turns intake answers into a draft script — this is the actual "product" logic worth iterating on as you learn what makes a good script. `lib/scriptSafetyCheckPrompt.js` builds the prompt (and parses the response) used for the automatic second-opinion check that decides whether a new signup can be auto-approved or needs your manual review. `lib/helpChatPrompt.js` is the system prompt for the small help-chat widget on the intake form. `lib/widgetChatPrompt.js` builds a paying client's own AI persona from their approved script, for their website widget. `lib/conversationInsightPrompt.js` builds the prompt (and parses the response) used to tag a finished website widget conversation as booked, an unbooked lead, a resolved question, or unclear. `lib/followUpPrompt.js` builds the prompt (and parses the response) used to draft a follow-up email for an unbooked lead, in that client's own tone. `lib/emailClient.js` sends the actual follow-up email via Resend's API, using plain `fetch` (no SDK), once a draft is approved. `lib/retellClient.js` pushes a paying client's approved script into Retell AI as a real, callable phone agent, using plain `fetch` (no SDK), triggered from the "Create phone agent" button on the dashboard. `lib/googleCalendarClient.js` handles the Google OAuth flow plus the actual freeBusy/event-creation calls behind real-time appointment booking, again with plain `fetch` and Node's built-in `Intl` for time-zone-correct datetimes -- no SDK, no date library. `lib/pastCustomerOutreachPrompt.js` builds the prompt (and parses the response) used to draft a repeat/seasonal-work reminder for one of a client's own past customers. `lib/csv.js` is a small hand-written CSV parser (quoted fields, embedded commas, escaped quotes) used to read an uploaded past-customer list without adding an npm dependency. `lib/db.js` is the JSON-file storage, for clients, widget conversations, and past customers -- also where a client's magic-link login tokens, portal token, and hosted-page slug are looked up and assigned. `server.js`'s client self-service portal section (everything under `/api/client-portal/*`, plus the public `/site/:slug` hosted-page route and `renderHostedSitePage`) is where a client's own login and hosted page are handled -- `clientPortalView` is the sanitized, credential-free projection of a client record every one of those routes returns. `public/` has the site's pages (landing, onboard, dashboard, success, widget-guide, customer-portal, client-login, client-portal) as plain HTML/CSS/JS — no framework, no build step. `public/client-login.html` is where a client requests their magic-link login. `public/client-portal.html` is their self-service account once logged in -- script, billing, phone agent, conversations, calendar, usage, and the hosted-page toggle, all scoped to just their own data. `public/widget-guide.html` is the client-facing, platform-by-platform "how do I add this to my website" guide, linked from the dashboard's widget code box. `public/customer-portal.html` is the client-facing, token-linked page where a paying client uploads their past-customer list and reviews/approves/sends their own AI-drafted reminders. `public/widget.js` is the embeddable chat widget clients paste into their own websites -- it's plain, dependency-free JavaScript on purpose, since it has to run safely on someone else's site alongside whatever else is already there.

See `LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md` for the non-technical, step-by-step path from this code to a real URL real customers can use.
